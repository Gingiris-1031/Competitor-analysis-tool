Why Teams Actually Leave Kompyte in 2026

Based on community discussions across r/SaaS, r/startups, and G2 reviews through Q2 2026 — the real exit reasons, not the polished sales objections.

🔀

"Semrush acquired Kompyte — roadmap is unclear post-acquisition"

The Semrush acquisition closed in October 2022 but full integration has been slow. Kompyte's standalone roadmap has shifted twice since then. Teams locking into multi-year contracts are betting on a product trajectory that Semrush hasn't publicly committed to. The enterprise CI tier isn't the place to absorb acquisition uncertainty.

💸

"Price starts at ~$1,000/month — no transparent pricing, enterprise-only"

Kompyte has never published pricing. Reported contracts anchor around $1,000/month minimum, with no self-serve tier, no trial, and no per-report option. For founders and sub-20-person teams, there's no on-ramp. The deal requires a sales call, a demo, and an annual commitment before you've validated the tool.

📊

"More alerts, less insight — Kompyte tells me what changed, not why competitors moved"

Kompyte is fundamentally a monitoring aggregator. It catches when a competitor updates a page, launches a feature, or publishes a press release — but it doesn't explain the strategic intent behind those moves. Teams increasingly want synthesis, not raw alerts. Knowing a competitor changed their pricing page is table stakes; understanding why they repositioned requires a teardown, not a notification.

📆

"No per-report or usage-based pricing — forces annual commitment from day one"

Competitor research is episodic — triggered by board meetings, launches, pricing changes. A monthly subscription model doesn't match how CI actually gets used. Kompyte's annual-commitment-only structure means you're paying for 12 months of access whether you run 3 reports or 300. Pay-per-report alternatives have taken share precisely because they fit real usage patterns.

🤖

"No LLM/AI workflow integration — no MCP, no Claude, no structured output"

In 2026, lean teams run competitive intelligence directly inside Claude or Cursor via MCP. Kompyte has no API for agentic workflows, no Claude plugin, and no structured data output for LLM consumption. For teams that have moved to AI-native stacks, this is a structural gap that Kompyte hasn't addressed — and given the Semrush acquisition uncertainty, may not address soon.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Kompyte still being developed after the Semrush acquisition?

Yes, Kompyte still operates as a standalone product at kompyte.com. Semrush has indicated integration will deepen over time but has not announced a deprecation timeline. Customers should monitor official communications before locking into multi-year contracts.

What is the best Kompyte alternative for small teams?

Analook for ad-hoc competitive teardowns ($5 per report or $19/month for 30 reports). VisualPing if you specifically need page-change monitoring ($14/month). Combined cost stays under $50/month — vs. Kompyte's typical $1,000+ entry.

Is Crayon better than Kompyte?

Crayon has stronger battlecard workflows, deeper Slack/Salesforce integrations, and more polished UI. Kompyte's strength was always speed-to-implementation. With the Semrush acquisition adding uncertainty to Kompyte's roadmap, Crayon is the safer enterprise choice in 2026.

Does Analook replace Crayon and Klue?

Only for the founder/small-team use case. Analook is a competitive teardown tool — fast, broad, cheap. Crayon and Klue are battlecard + sales enablement platforms — built for ongoing team workflows at 50+ person orgs. Different tier, different use case.

What pricing does Kompyte publish?

Kompyte does not publish pricing publicly. Reported customer contracts typically start around $1,000/month, scaling with seat counts and competitor monitoring volume. Always negotiate; the published tier structure is a starting point, not a ceiling.
